Continual Pretraining of World Action Models from Human Egocentric and Robot Data

How does the model keep learning?

Human videos show how people manipulate objects, while robot trajectories supply executable actions. Video and action experts exchange information through joint attention and learn future frames and continuous actions with flow matching.

Data and memory

Atom-Cur checks image quality, motion consistency and alignment between video and instructions. The two pretraining stages use about 6,100 hours from 14 datasets, with historical replay accounting for 15% of the duration at each stage.

For long tasks, event memory pairs subtask descriptions with key observations. Reversed sequences teach a progress estimator to recognize when an operation has moved back to an earlier state.

Evaluation scope

We evaluated on four simulation benchmarks and 18 physical tasks across Piper, ARX and Franka platforms.

Physical task examples

Three selected dataset episodes. Piper and ARX use a head camera; Franka uses a fixed external camera.
